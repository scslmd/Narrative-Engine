Studio Desk — Layout Options

Three layout concepts for the single-writing-workspace redesign

Option A — Classic Three-Panel

Faithful to design doc. Left rail navigates project artifacts. Center editor is the stable work surface. Right context panel stacks Ideas / Characters / World / Relationships / Generation / Review / Inspect as tabs. Bottom tray shows jobs, suggestions, and lineage. Best for deep, multi-panel work.

It was not the storm that changed Mara. Storms came and went on the coast; she had learned to read them the way her grandmother read tea leaves — for what they promised, not what they delivered.
What changed her was the silence after. Three days of glass-flat sea and sky that matched too perfectly, the kind of calm that makes fishermen check their nets twice and mothers pull their children closer.
She stood at the edge of the wharf, the salt-crusted rail biting into her palms, and watched the horizon where the weatherman said nothing was coming. Her grandmother would have smiled at that. The sea never sends nothing.
Kael found her there at dusk, his shadow stretching long across the weathered planks. "You've been standing there since morning," he said, and she could hear it — the question underneath the observation, the one he never asked directly.
"I'm waiting," she told him, and for the first time she meant it as an answer, not a deflection.
The gulls had stopped crying. That was what she noticed first when it came — not the wind, not the darkening water, but the absence of birds. The sky emptied in minutes, the way a room empties when someone walks in who changes everything.

Option B — Full-Width Editor + Bottom Dock

Editor gets maximum vertical space. Support panels slide up from a collapsed bottom dock. Left rail and context panels coexist in the dock row. Best for distraction-free drafting with tools available on demand. Ideal for writers who want the editor to dominate.

Kael found her there at dusk, his shadow stretching long across the weathered planks. "You've been standing there since morning," he said, and she could hear it — the question underneath the observation, the one he never asked directly.
"I'm waiting," she told him, and for the first time she meant it as an answer, not a deflection.
The gulls had stopped crying. That was what she noticed first when it came — not the wind, not the darkening water, but the absence of birds. The sky emptied in minutes, the way a room empties when someone walks in who changes everything.
The first wave hit the pilings with a sound like a fist against a door. Mara didn't flinch. She had been waiting for this exact moment since she was old enough to understand that the sea keeps promises — just not the ones people want to hear.
Behind her, Kael's hand found her shoulder. Not touching. Close enough that she felt the warmth of it, the intention. "Mara," he said, and it wasn't the question he usually carried. It was something else. Something that sounded like a warning, or maybe a blessing.

Option C — Floating Workspace

Panels are independent floating windows within the workspace. Command bar stays anchored top. Panels can be repositioned, resized, and hidden. A floating toolbar at bottom provides quick panel toggles. Most flexible, supports multi-monitor-style layouts. Best for power users who want custom arrangements.

Comparison

Criteria A: Classic B: Full-Width C: Floating
Editor space Constrained (3 columns) Maximum (full width) Flexible (user-controlled)
Panel accessibility Always visible (right panel) Dock toggle (1 click) Floating toolbar (1 click)
Cognitive load Low (fixed layout) Lowest (minimal chrome) High (user arranges)
Multi-panel support Single right panel + tabs Single dock panel + tabs Multiple floating panels
Implementation effort Low (grid layout) Low (flex + toggle) High (drag, resize, persist)
Best for Research-backed default Drafting-focused writers Power users, large screens
